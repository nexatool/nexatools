What Is a PDF Watermark?

A watermark is text or an image placed across the pages of a document, usually semi-transparent, so it doesn't block the underlying content but still stays visible. On PDFs, watermarks are commonly used to label a document as "CONFIDENTIAL," "DRAFT," or "SAMPLE," or to mark ownership before sharing a file with someone outside your organization.

Unlike a header or footer, a watermark typically sits behind or across the main content, often at an angle, making it hard to miss without covering up the text underneath. Once a watermark is added and the file is saved, it becomes a permanent part of that page's content.
