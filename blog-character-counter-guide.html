Character Counter Guide: Fit Your Text Into Twitter, Meta & SMS Limits

📅 August 9, 2026 ⏱ 5 min read ✍️ NexaTools Team

Some limits are measured in words. Others are measured in characters and the difference matters more than people realize. A tweet, an SMS, and a search engine snippet all cut you off by character, not by word, which means a handful of long words can eat your limit far faster than expected.

This guide walks through how character limits work across different platforms, and how to check your text instantly for free using NexaTools.

Why Character Limits Are Different From Word Limits

A word counter tells you how many words you've written. A character counter tells you how much visible space your text takes up including every letter, number, space and punctuation mark. Platforms with a fixed input field, like a text message or a search result snippet, care about characters because that's what physically fits on screen. Writing "internationalization" and "cat" both count as one word, but they take up very different amounts of space which is exactly why character-based limits exist.

Twitter / X Posts
Capped at 280 characters. Links, mentions and hashtags all count toward the total, so it's easy to run out of room faster than expected.
Meta Descriptions
Search engines typically display around 150-160 characters before truncating with an ellipsis, cutting off anything written after that point.
SMS Messages
A single SMS segment is limited to 160 characters. Go over, and the message either gets truncated or split into multiple segments depending on the carrier.

Where Character Limits Trip People Up

  • Forgetting links count too a shortened URL still eats into a character limit on most platforms
  • Emoji and special characters some platforms count certain emoji as more than one character behind the scenes
  • Writing the headline first, checking the limit last it's faster to check the limit while drafting rather than rewriting after the fact
  • Assuming every platform uses the same number 160 characters is common for SMS and meta descriptions, but Twitter/X uses 280, so don't assume one limit fits everywhere

SEO Writers: A Quick Checklist

  • Keep meta descriptions under roughly 155-160 characters so search engines don't cut them off mid-sentence
  • Keep page titles under roughly 55-60 characters for the same reason
  • Front-load the most important information in case of truncation on smaller screens
  • Recheck character count after any last-minute edits a few extra words can push you over without you noticing
