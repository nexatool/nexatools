How to Send a Professional Invoice as a Freelancer in Pakistan — Complete Guide 2026

📅 June 14, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ NexaTools Team
Reviewed by Rashid Amin, Founder of NexaTools · Last Updated: July 2026
🧾

One of the biggest challenges for Pakistani freelancers — especially those working on Upwork, Fiverr, or with direct clients — is getting paid properly and on time. A professional invoice is not just a payment request. It is a document that protects you, builds trust with your client, and makes your freelance business look serious.

This guide covers everything you need to know about creating and sending invoices as a Pakistani freelancer, whether your client is in Lahore, London, or Los Angeles.

Why Pakistani Freelancers Need Proper Invoices

Many freelancers in Pakistan simply send a WhatsApp message saying "please send payment" and then wonder why clients delay or dispute amounts. A proper invoice solves all of this:

  • Creates a written record of the work done and amount agreed
  • Gives the client clear payment instructions and a deadline
  • Makes your business look professional and trustworthy
  • Helps you track income for tax purposes
  • Reduces payment disputes — everything is in writing
  • Often required by international clients for their own accounting records

What Every Freelance Invoice Must Include

Invoice Number
Every invoice needs a unique number — INV-001, INV-002, and so on. This helps both you and your client track payments. Never send two invoices with the same number.
Your Name and Contact Details
Your full name or business name, email address, phone number, and city. For international clients, include your country (Pakistan). If you have a website or portfolio, add that too.
Client Name and Details
The full name or company name of the client, their email, and their country. This makes the invoice clearer and shows professionalism.
Invoice Date and Due Date
Always include both. Invoice date is when you issued it. Due date is when payment is expected. Many Pakistani freelancers use Net 7 (7 days) or Net 15 (15 days). International clients often expect Net 30.
Services Description
List exactly what work you did. "Freelance work" is not enough. Write: "Logo design — 3 concepts, 2 revision rounds" or "WordPress website development — 5 pages with contact form." The more specific, the fewer disputes.
Amount and Currency
State the currency clearly — PKR, USD, GBP, EUR. For international clients, always invoice in USD or their local currency. Confusion about currency can lead to underpayment.
Payment Method
Tell the client exactly how to pay. Include your bank account details, Easypaisa/JazzCash number, PayPal email, Wise account, or Payoneer details — whichever applies.
Total Amount Due
Make the total bold and clear. If there are multiple line items, show subtotal, any taxes or discounts, and the final total separately.

Payment Methods for Pakistani Freelancers

This is where Pakistani freelancers have unique challenges. Not all international payment methods are available in Pakistan. Here is what commonly works:

✦ For International Clients (USD/EUR/GBP)
Payoneer — widely used by Pakistani freelancers. Accepted by Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer. Withdraw to your Pakistani bank account in PKR.

Wise (formerly TransferWise) — a common option for direct clients, generally offering fast transfers and transparent fees.

PayPal — limited in Pakistan. You can receive payments but withdrawal options are restricted. Use with caution.

Crypto (USDT) — used by some Pakistani freelancers for direct international payments, though it carries its own risks and regulatory considerations.
✦ For Local Pakistani Clients (PKR)
Bank Transfer — a professional option. Share your account title, account number, and IBAN.

Easypaisa / JazzCash — fast and convenient for smaller amounts.

Raast — Pakistan's instant payment system. Share your Raast ID for instant transfers between banks.

How to Handle Taxes on Your Invoice

Tax rules for freelancers in Pakistan can change and depend on your specific situation, so this section is general guidance rather than tax advice:

  • FBR registration — freelancers earning above certain income thresholds are generally required to file a tax return in Pakistan; check current FBR rules for the applicable threshold
  • Export of services — income from foreign clients is often treated as export income, which can carry different tax treatment under Pakistani law
  • Withholding tax — some Pakistani clients may deduct withholding tax; ask them to provide a tax deduction certificate
  • Sales tax / GST — treatment varies by service type and province, so consult a local tax advisor for your specific situation

Common Invoicing Mistakes Pakistani Freelancers Make

  • No due date — without a due date, clients feel no urgency to pay. Always include one.
  • Vague service description — "Design work" is not enough. Be specific about exactly what was delivered.
  • Wrong currency — always confirm currency with the client before starting work, and state it clearly on the invoice.
  • No invoice number — makes tracking payments difficult. Always number your invoices.
  • Sending invoice as an image — send as PDF instead. Images can be edited and look less professional.
  • No follow-up — if payment is not received by the due date, send a polite reminder within a few days.
  • No late fee clause — consider stating a late payment fee to encourage on-time payment.

How to Follow Up on Unpaid Invoices

Late payments are common in freelancing, both locally and internationally. Here is how to handle them professionally:

✦ Follow Up Email Template
Subject: Payment Reminder — Invoice #INV-001

Hi [Client Name],

I hope you are doing well. I wanted to follow up on Invoice #INV-001 for [service] dated [date], which was due on [due date]. The total amount outstanding is [amount].

Please let me know if you have any questions or if there is anything I can help clarify. I would appreciate payment at your earliest convenience.

Thank you,
[Your Name]

A common approach is to send the first reminder a day or two after the due date. If there's no response after a week, send a second reminder with a firmer tone. If the invoice remains unpaid after that, consider escalating — especially for international clients, where you may need to open a dispute on the platform.
